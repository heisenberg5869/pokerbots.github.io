EV = p × win + (1 − p) × loss. Variance = E[(payoff − EV)²]. The law of large numbers describes the average settling near EV as sample size grows.

2. Pot odds and bluff calculator

Predict: what fraction must fold for a pot-sized bluff to break even?

Equity needed to call:
Call cost ÷ (pot + opponent bet + call cost)

Bluff must get folds:
Bluff size ÷ (pot + bluff size)

Minimum defense frequency: Pot ÷ (pot + bet)

Pot definition: the displayed pot is the amount in the middle before the opponent bets. A call wins that pot plus the opponent's bet; a bluff risks its size to win the current pot.

Posterior(type | action) = prior(type) × P(action | type) ÷ P(action). A type becomes more likely when it both was plausible beforehand and often takes the observed action.

Quick self-check

Choose one answer for each question, then check your score. Explanations appear after grading.

1. A pot-sized zero-equity bluff needs the opponent to fold how often to break even?

A pot-sized bluff risks P to win P, so it breaks even when folds and calls are equally likely.

2. You hold one ace. How many pocket-ace combinations remain?

Three aces remain, and choosing two gives C(3,2) = 3 combos.

3. A posterior probability after observing a bet depends on…

Bayes' rule multiplies the prior by the chance of seeing the action for that type, then normalizes.

4. Against uniform rock-paper-scissors, which pure action has the highest expected payoff?

Each pure action wins, loses, and ties with equal probability, for expected payoff zero.
